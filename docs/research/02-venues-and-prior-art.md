### A.2 Recommendation for the ~9-week plan (17 Sep → ~19 Nov 2026)

**Primary: MIDL 2027 full paper, Validation Studies track if it runs again (otherwise the main track).**
- Estimated deadline **~early Dec 2026** (2026 edition: 5 Dec 2025, extended to 10 Dec). A 9-week plan finishes ~19 Nov, which leaves about 2 weeks of buffer for writing and internal review.
- Why it fits: MIDL explicitly accepts validation and evaluation studies and allows 10–14 pages, enough room for multi-dataset alignment metrics plus stress tests. Its community (deep learning for medical imaging, strong interest in shortcuts and XAI) is the right audience for "fusion changes where the model looks". A modest accuracy gain is not disqualifying there.
- Action: watch https://2027.midl.io/ for the call for papers (normally posted Sep–Oct). If the deadline lands in late November, cut Weeks 8–9 short.

**Fallback 1 — if results slip past early December: MICCAI 2027 main conference, estimated ~late Feb 2027.** That is about 3 more months. Frame the paper as methodology (the alignment-scoring protocol) plus analysis. The 8-page limit forces the paper to focus on derm + one CXR experiment. Double-blind review with a rebuttal.
**Fallback 2 — if MICCAI rejects (early decisions ~May 2027): iMIMIC 2027 or MultiTab 2027 workshops (estimated ~late Jun / early Jul 2027).** These are the best topical fits and are LNCS-indexed. A MIDL 2027 short paper (estimated mid-Apr 2027) could also secure an early flag-plant. CHIL 2027 (estimated early Feb 2027, Track 2) is an alternative to MICCAI if the paper grows into an evaluation study with a stronger clinical framing.

**Not recommended**
- **ISBI 2027 (26 Oct 2026, confirmed):** only 5.5 weeks away, and 4 pages cannot hold the multi-dataset evaluation. Consider it only as a deliberate early short version of the derm result (PAD-UFES-20 + Bissoto traps). ISBI is single-blind and allows arXiv, so it would not block a later MIDL or MICCAI extension. Still, dual-submission and overlap rules must be checked.
- **SPIE MI 2027:** abstracts closed 5 Aug 2026 (late submissions need chair approval).
- **IPMI 2027:** wants theory and method papers.
- **ML4H 2026:** closed.

### B.2 Answers to the three prior-art questions

**(1) Metadata + image (cross-)attention fusion on skin datasets: this is well established, not novel.**
MetaNet (2020), MetaBlock (2021), MAT (2021), transformer fusion (Cheslerean-Boghiu 2023), and in 2026 an almost identical design on PAD-UFES-20 (Mridha & Islam, medRxiv).

The size of the gain depends on the dataset:
- **PAD-UFES-20 (clinical smartphone images):** gains are usually *large*, ~7–10 BACC points (Pacheco & Krohling 2020: ~7%; secondary sources report +10.4 BACC).
- **Dermoscopic ISIC/HAM:** gains are ~2–5 points (ISIC 2019 +2.2 per the same secondary source; HAM10000 +3.9 to +4.75).
- **Cross-attention specifically on PAD-UFES-20:** DualRefNet reports no gain from cross-attention (68.99% BACC). Mridha & Islam report only +0.004 AUC.

So an expected **+2.5% gain on PAD-UFES-20 sits below typical MetaBlock-style gains**. That is fine given the "not accuracy" framing, but reviewers will compare against 0.70–0.80 BACC baselines (MetaBlock-SE 70.2; PiT+MetaBlock 0.800; DualRefNet 0.845). Report a MetaBlock baseline so the fusion model is not seen as a weak implementation.

**(2) Quantitative scoring of multimodal-model saliency/attention against human annotations: no paper found that does this.**
Every skin fusion paper found (items 3, 6, 8, 9, 11–15) either offers no explanations or shows Grad-CAM/attention *qualitatively*. CheXlocalize-style quantitative benchmarking exists only for image-only models. The July 2026 npj scoping review (82 studies) independently concludes that multimodal XAI evaluation is mostly qualitative. Two items could not be fully verified from their full text: **Ketabi et al. 2023 (MICCAI-W)** and **Atiq & Fattah 2025 (arXiv)**.

**(3) Shortcut analysis of metadata-fused models (Bissoto trap sets, chest-drain stratification): no paper found that does this.**
Trap sets, NEATX and "Mask of truth" all study image-only models. MedCLIP probing (Aug 2026) studies a vision encoder, not tabular fusion. No work was found asking whether metadata fusion reduces or amplifies artifact/drain shortcuts.

### B.3 Does the gap statement hold?

> "Existing fusion papers report accuracy with at most qualitative explanations; none scores fusion attention against human annotation."

**It largely holds as of 2026-09-17, with caveats.**
1. **Change "none" to a hedged claim** such as "to our knowledge, no prior work quantitatively…". Two papers (Ketabi et al. 2023; Atiq & Fattah 2025) could not be fully read and may contain mask-overlap numbers. Read both before submitting.
2. **Do not claim the architecture as a contribution.** Metadata→image cross-attention on PAD-UFES-20 was already published (Mridha & Islam, medRxiv 2026) and shown qualitatively. Position the contribution as *the evaluation protocol and findings*: alignment scoring plus shortcut stress tests of fusion vs image-only.
3. **Include the shortcut angle in the gap statement.** It is arguably the stronger novelty ("no prior work tests whether metadata fusion changes shortcut reliance under trap sets / drain stratification").
4. **Data-design caveat (affects the claim, not the novelty).** The human annotations and the metadata do not naturally sit on the same images:
   - **PAD-UFES-20** has no lesion masks and no concept labels, so it cannot be scored directly against human annotations.
   - **SkinCon** (Fitzpatrick17k / DDI) has concepts but little patient metadata beyond skin type.
   - **CheXlocalize** (CheXpert) does have age, sex and view position.
   - **Bissoto trap sets** use ISIC images, which have age, sex and site metadata.
   - **NEATX** uses NIH-CXR14, which has age, sex and view position.
   
   The "fusion changes where the model looks, scored against humans" claim therefore rests on ISIC (masks + artifact labels) and CheXpert/NIH rather than PAD-UFES-20. State this explicitly, or annotate a PAD-UFES-20 subset.

### B.4 Top novelty threats (ranked)

1. **Mridha & Islam, medRxiv 2026**: same architecture on the same dataset, attention maps shown qualitatively. It is the direct competitor on "cross-attention fusion on PAD-UFES-20".
2. **Ketabi et al., MICCAI 2023 Workshops**: "improving … explainability" of multimodal CXR. Quantitative evaluation is unverified and it could overlap with the CheXlocalize arm.
3. **Atiq & Fattah, arXiv 2510.17773**: explainable metadata + segmentation fusion with masks available. Quantitative scoring is unverified.
4. **Cheslerean-Boghiu et al., arXiv 2304.14505**: claims native image and metadata interpretability for transformer fusion.
5. **Pedersen / Sourget / Cheplygina (PURRlab), arXiv 2608.12086**: active NEATX shortcut work. Not fusion yet, but a group well placed to publish the fusion + drain analysis first.
